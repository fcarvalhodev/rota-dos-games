Games Route

Who did the most for games in Brazil?

Grants, laws, taxes and credit for people who make games, from 2003 to today: what each government did, how much money went out and what is only an announcement. With the same standard for everyone.

📊 See the summary in icons

The short answer

Each government did a piece. Lula, in his first term, launched the first federal grant call for games, in 2004. Under Dilma, games became eligible for the Rouanet Law in 2011, and Ancine's first grant call was announced; it came out under Temer: R$10 million for 23 games. Bolsonaro cut the federal tax on consoles from 50% to 20%. Lula, in his third term, signed the Games Legal Framework, which came from a bill by deputy Kim Kataguiri, and regulated it on October 14, 2026, with R$200 million from Finep. The number of studios in the country went from 375 to 1,009 between 2018 and 2022.

Four ways to help games

Before comparing governments, it helps to know that “helping games” can mean very different things:

💰Direct moneyGrant calls that pay for game production, like Ancine's
🧾Lower taxesLike the cut in the IPI on consoles. It makes the device cheaper, but does not pay those who make games
📜LawRules that recognize the sector and open doors, like the Legal Framework
🏦Credit and trainingLoans, courses, labs and fairs

2003 – 2010
  • JogosBR, 2004. The federal government's first grant call to fund game production in the country, launched at the Ministry of Culture. Documented
  • Little beyond that. We found no other federal policy just for games in the period. Documented

Dilma Rousseff

2011 – May 2016
  • Rouanet Law opened to games, 2011. Game projects become able to raise sponsorship with a discount on companies' income tax, like films. Documented
  • BNDES enters the sector, 2013 to 2015. It sponsors the BIG festival from 2013, funds a USP study that mapped the sector and invests in two studios through funds. The bank's credit lines, however, had never been used by game companies. Documented
  • First Ancine grant call announced, 2015. Ancine and the Ministry of Culture announce a call for about 20 games featuring Brazilian culture, with money from the Audiovisual Sector Fund, which comes from the sector itself and not from the Treasury. Documented

Jair Bolsonaro

2019 – 2022
  • Console tax cut three times. The IPI on video games falls from 50% to 40% in August 2019, to 30% in October 2020 and to 20% in August 2021. Handheld devices fall from 20% to zero. The government said it was to encourage the sector. Documented
  • Little new money for those who make games. We found no new federal grant call just for games in the period. Documented

Lula, 3rd term

2023 – today
  • Games Legal Framework, May 2024. Lula signs Law 14.852, which came from a bill by deputy Kim Kataguiri, of União, approved by the Chamber and the Senate. The law recognizes games as culture and as research and innovation, opens the Rouanet Law and the Audiovisual Law, gives special treatment to studios with revenue of up to R$16 million a year and leaves betting out. Documented
  • Regulation and package, October 14, 2026. Eleven days before the runoff, Lula signs the decree regulating the law and announces: R$200 million from Finep for early-stage game companies and other digital solutions, cheaper credit in the Audiovisual Sector Fund, the public Game Brasil platform and game courses at five federal universities. Documented
  • Part of it is a promise for 2027. Two Ministry of Culture lines expect to raise R$50 million each in 2027, and an MCTI program expects R$100 million of private money in 2027 and 2028. The changes to Ancine's credit still need approval. Open

Is it true?

True

“Bolsonaro lowered the tax on video games.” The IPI on consoles fell from 50% to 20% in three decrees, from 2019 to 2021.

Half true

“Lula created the Games Legal Framework.” He signed the law in 2024 and regulated it in 2026. But the bill is by deputy Kim Kataguiri, of União, and it was approved by Congress.

False

“Before 2024, the federal government never helped games.” There was the JogosBR call in 2004, the Rouanet Law opened to games in 2011 and R$10 million from Ancine for 23 games in 2017.

False

“Games can only use the Rouanet Law now, under Lula.” They have been able to since 2011. The 2024 Legal Framework reinforced this, and the 2026 package created support to help projects raise funds.

Half true

“The government will give R$200 million to games.” It is a Finep call for early-stage companies in games and other digital solutions, not only games. Other lines are for 2027 and depend on fundraising.

True

“The Legal Framework does not apply to betting.” The law says games with bets, prizes or random outcomes are not electronic games and do not receive the benefits.

Not shown

“The October package was made to win the election.” It came out 11 days before the runoff, and part of it is a promise for 2027. But the law is from 2024, and a date does not prove intent.

In short

  • Federal support for games began with Lula in 2004 and grew little by little under Dilma and Temer, with the Rouanet Law and the Ancine call.
  • Bolsonaro bet on lower taxes on consoles, which helps those who buy, not those who make games.
  • Lula 3 made the biggest change in the rules, the Legal Framework, which came from an opposition deputy, and the largest package announced. Part of it is still a promise.
  • Studios nearly tripled between 2018 and 2022, but that has many causes beyond the government.

Sources and limits of this reading

This route lists federal measures found in news reports, laws and official documents. It is not a complete inventory: states and cities have their own grant calls, and there may be smaller support that never made the news. Grant values are the announced amounts; we did not check how much was actually paid. The studio count comes from Abragames surveys whose methods changed over time. A sector that grows under a government does not prove it grew because of it.
